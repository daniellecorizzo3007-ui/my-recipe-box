Serving Timeline

Best Flavour — 24 Hours Ahead

  1. Make dough.
  2. Cover and refrigerate 24 hours.
  3. Portion cold dough.
  4. Bake directly from chilled.

Freezer Option

  1. Make and portion dough balls.
  2. Freeze until firm, then store airtight.
  3. Bake from frozen, allowing a little extra baking time if needed.
  4. Finish with flaky sea salt.

Ingredients — 12 Large Cookies

A) Brown Butter & Toasted Milk Powder

  • 200 g unsalted butter
  • 15 g full-cream milk powder

B) Sugars & Wet Ingredients

  • 130 g white sugar
  • 120 g light brown sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 extra egg yolk
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract

C) Dry Ingredients

  • 280 g plain flour
  • 10 g cornstarch / gustin
  • ¾ tsp baking soda
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • ¾ tsp fine salt
  • 1½ tsp espresso powder

D) Chocolate

  • 250 g total chocolate
  • Recommended: approximately 125 g milk chocolate + 125 g dark chocolate
  • For the best bakery texture: use approximately 150 g chopped chocolate and 100 g chocolate chips across the milk/dark mix
  • Extra chocolate pieces for the tops, optional

F) Finish

  • Flaky sea salt

Advanced Preparation

  • Use a digital scale for the most consistent results.
  • Bring the egg and yolk close to room temperature before mixing.
  • Chop part of the chocolate into irregular pieces so the cookies develop both fine chocolate shards and larger molten pockets.
  • Plan for a 24-hour dough rest whenever possible.
  • Do not add vanilla while browning the butter; add it later with the egg and yolk so its aroma is preserved.

Method

STEP A1 — Melt the Butter

Place the 200 g unsalted butter in a light-coloured saucepan over medium heat. Melt completely.

STEP A2 — Add the Milk Powder

Once the butter is melted and beginning to foam, sprinkle in the 15 g milk powder. Whisk or stir constantly so it disperses through the butter rather than clumping.

STEP A3 — Brown & Toast

Continue cooking over medium to medium-low heat. Stir frequently, then constantly as the milk solids begin to colour. Cook until the butter smells deeply nutty and the milk solids are a rich golden-hazelnut brown. Remove from the heat immediately before they become too dark.

Important: Keep every browned milk-solid speck. Do not strain them out.

STEP A4 — Cool

Pour the browned butter and all toasted milk solids into a large mixing bowl. Cool for approximately 20–30 minutes, until warm rather than hot and slightly thickened.

For this revised version, do not discard the toasted mixture to force it back to the old 170 g browned-butter target. The extra toasted milk solids are intentional.

STEP B1 — Add the Sugars

Add the 130 g white sugar and 120 g light brown sugar to the cooled browned butter. Whisk for 1–2 minutes until glossy, evenly combined and slightly thickened.

STEP B2 — Add Egg, Yolk & Vanilla

Add the whole egg, extra egg yolk and 2 tsp vanilla extract. Whisk until smooth, glossy and fully emulsified.

STEP C1 — Combine the Dry Ingredients

In a separate bowl, whisk together the plain flour, cornstarch, baking soda, baking powder, fine salt and espresso powder until evenly distributed.

STEP D1 — Make the Dough

Add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture. Fold gently with a spatula until only a few streaks of flour remain. Do not overmix.

STEP D2 — Add the Chocolate

Fold in the 250 g chocolate until evenly distributed. The finished dough should be soft, thick and slightly sticky.

STEP E1 — Rest the Dough

Cover tightly and refrigerate for at least 4 hours if necessary, but 12 hours is preferred and 24 hours gives the best bakery-style flavour and texture. The dough may be rested up to around 48 hours.

STEP E2 — Portion

Divide the dough into 12 large portions. Roll gently into tall, rough dough balls rather than perfectly smooth balls. Press a few extra chocolate pieces into the tops if desired.

STEP F1 — Preheat

Preheat the oven to 180°C fan or 190°C conventional. Line a baking tray with baking paper. Leave generous room between cookies.

STEP F2 — Bake

Bake for approximately 11–14 minutes. The edges should be golden and set while the centres still look slightly underdone. Do not wait for the centres to look fully baked in the oven.

STEP F3 — Bakery Finish

Immediately after baking, lightly bang the tray once on the bench to encourage a wrinkled bakery-style surface. If necessary, use a round cutter or glass to gently scoot the still-soft cookies into neat circles.

STEP F4 — Salt & Cool

While still hot, add a very small pinch of flaky sea salt to each cookie. Leave on the tray for approximately 10 minutes before transferring to a rack. The cookies will continue setting as they cool.

Bakery Notes

  • The milk powder is now part of the core recipe, not an optional upgrade.
  • Toasting the milk powder with the butter boosts caramelised, nutty dairy flavour.
  • Vanilla is deliberately added after the butter cools rather than during browning.
  • Espresso powder should deepen the chocolate flavour without making the cookie taste strongly of coffee.
  • The revised 130 g white / 120 g brown sugar balance gives slightly more chew and caramel depth while preserving crisp edges.
  • A combination of chopped chocolate and chips gives both molten chocolate pools and defined chocolate pieces.

Storage, Freezing & Serving

  • Room temperature: Store airtight for approximately 3–4 days.
  • Freezing dough: Freeze portioned dough balls airtight for up to 3 months.
  • Serving: Best eaten slightly warm, when the chocolate is soft.
  • Microwave: Warm a baked cookie for approximately 8–12 seconds.
  • Oven: Reheat at approximately 150°C for 4–6 minutes.

Troubleshooting

  • Cookies spread too much: Dough or browned butter was likely too warm. Chill thoroughly before baking.
  • Cookies are too thick: Allow the chilled dough to sit briefly at room temperature before baking or press the portions slightly flatter.
  • Cookies are dry: They were likely overbaked. Remove while the centres still appear slightly underdone.
  • Milk solids burn: Lower the heat and stir continuously once the butter begins to colour.
  • Strong coffee flavour: Reduce espresso powder slightly next batch; it is intended to support the chocolate rather than dominate it.

Final Recipe Standard

This version keeps the successful original bakery-style cookie structure while permanently incorporating toasted milk powder, standardising the espresso powder, preserving the vanilla aroma, slightly rebalancing the sugars, and making flaky sea salt part of the final finish.
